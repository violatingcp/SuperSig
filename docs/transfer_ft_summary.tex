\begin{abstract}
We evaluate six contrastive/regularized pretraining objectives --- three
unsupervised (SimCLR, SIGReg-SSL/LeJEPA, unsupervised NPLM) and three
supervised (SupCon, SupCon+SIGReg, supervised NPLM) --- as \emph{end-to-end
fine-tuning} losses for ViT-B/16 trunks on four transfer datasets
(Stanford~Cars, Flowers-102, DTD, Galaxy10~DECals), each from three
pretrained bases (DINO, LeJEPA, VISReg): a $4\times3\times6$ grid.  Every
space is scored with an open-world novelty battery (held-out--class linear
probe, nearest-centroid accuracy, distance-based novelty AUCs, and
dataset-level anomaly-detection power), before and after an iterated
anchor-\emph{discovery} phase, and finally under \emph{residual}
fine-tuning.  Three headline results: (i) discovery, which is
probe-negative on frozen trunks, becomes probe-positive in 50/72 grid
cells once the trunk is fine-tuned end-to-end --- and the
SupCon-ft$+$discovery pipeline is positive in 12/12 dataset$\times$base
cells; (ii) the champion pre-discovery objective is regime-determined and
base-invariant (SupCon+SIGReg on fine-grained data, SimCLR on textures,
SupCon on few-class coarse data, supervised NPLM for calibration at few
classes); (iii) residual fine-tuning beats the discovery pipeline in all
12 cells, with the NPLM residual concat uniquely raising the probe
\emph{while retaining} calibrated per-event power --- the first cars
space to break the probe--calibration dissociation; (iv) a final
discovery pass on the residual winners is \emph{purity-gated}: it stacks
only where the residual space separates the novelty, yielding the
program's final records --- aircraft 0.863, cars 0.855, flowers 0.906,
DTD 0.862, Galaxy10 0.975 --- every one a residual pipeline; (v) the
same recipe transfers back to CIFAR (exps 73--74): supcon$\to$res concat
sets a new CIFAR-100 probe record (0.9594) and ties the CIFAR-10
pre-discovery record (0.9536), and discovery on these concats is again
a calibration repair at low purity, not a probe booster.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Fine-tuning recipe (exp-49/62 convention).}
Model $=$ pretrained trunk $+$ projection head
$768\!\to\!256\!\to\!100$; everything trainable.  Adam with cosine
schedule, trunk lr $10^{-5}$, head lr $10^{-3}$, AMP fp16, 20 epochs,
batch 32 with two augmented views (RandomResizedCrop, flip, jitter at
224\,px).  Per-arm seeds.  All objectives operate on the raw 100-D head
output $\z$; only softmax-contrastive losses $\ell_2$-normalize
internally.

\section{Loss functions}

Let $\z_i = f_\theta(\x_i)\in\R^{100}$, with two views per image stacked
into a $2N$ batch; $\hat\z = \z/\lVert\z\rVert$.

\subsection{Core primitives}

\paragraph{SIGReg (sliced Gaussianity).}
The repo's member of the VISReg/LeJEPA family: a sliced 1-D
Wasserstein-2 distance to $\mathcal N(0,I)$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SIG}}(\z_{1:B}) \;=\;
\frac1S \sum_{s=1}^{S}\;
\frac1B \sum_{b=1}^{B}
\bigl( \operatorname{sort}_b(\langle \z, u_s\rangle) - \Phi^{-1}(q_b) \bigr)^2,
\qquad u_s \sim \mathrm{Unif}(\mathbb S^{d-1}),
\end{equation}
with $S{=}64$ slices and $\Phi^{-1}(q_b)$ the standard-normal quantiles.
A \emph{classwise} variant applies the same functional per class to
$\z-\mmu_c$, pulling class $c$ toward $\mathcal N(\mmu_c, I)$ (used in the
CIFAR program; not part of the transfer grid below).

\paragraph{Softmax contrastive (NT-Xent / SupCon).}
For positives $P(i)$ and temperature $\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{con}} = \sum_i \frac{-1}{|P(i)|} \sum_{p\in P(i)}
\log \frac{\exp(\langle\hat\z_i,\hat\z_p\rangle/\tau)}
{\sum_{a\neq i}\exp(\langle\hat\z_i,\hat\z_a\rangle/\tau)} .
\end{equation}
With instance positives (the other view) and $\tau{=}0.5$ this is
SimCLR/NT-Xent; with class positives and $\tau{=}0.1$ it is SupCon.

\subsection{The hybrid contrastive cube (NPLM losses)}

\texttt{HybridContrastiveLoss} factorizes a contrastive objective into
\emph{positives} $\times$ \emph{critic} $\times$ \emph{estimator}
$\times$ \emph{marginal}, $\;\mathcal L = \mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}(g) +
\lambda\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{marg}}(\z)$:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{critic} $g(\z,\z')$: cosine
$\langle\hat\z,\hat\z'\rangle/\tau$;\; bilinear
$\langle\z,\z'\rangle/\tau$;\; distance
$-\tfrac1{2\tau}\lVert\z-\z'\rVert^2$.
\item \textbf{estimator}: \emph{softmax} (self-normalized per row, as
above --- recovers pointwise mutual information only up to a per-row
constant), or \emph{NPLM}: the un-normalized maximum-likelihood objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NPLM}}
= \E_{\mathrm{ref}}\!\left[e^{\,g}-1\right] - \E_{\mathrm{pos}}\!\left[g\right],
\label{eq:nplm}
\end{equation}
whose minimizer is the \emph{absolute} log density ratio
$g^\ast=\log \frac{p(\x,\x')}{p(\x)p(\x')}$ with the calibration property
$\E_{\mathrm{ref}}[e^{g^\ast}]=1$ (verified empirically, $r>0.9$ against
analytic PMI).  The reference set excludes positives and the diagonal;
the exponent is clamped at 30 (row-max subtraction would destroy
calibration).  At initialization the interaction term is
$\mathcal O(10^{10})$, so $\lambda$ is inert early and only shapes the
endgame; $\tau=1$ is a sharp optimum for the bilinear critic.
\item \textbf{marginal}: none, global SIGReg, or classwise SIGReg.
\end{itemize}
Calibrated NPLM spaces carry \emph{absolute} distance information (a
ready-to-use novelty metric), where softmax spaces are the better linear
probe inputs --- the program's central probe--calibration dissociation.

\subsection{Residual objectives (exp 71)}

Given a trained supervised \emph{parent} with frozen seen-class centroids
$\mmu_y$ in head space, a deepcopy is fine-tuned end-to-end on the
residual $\mathbf r = \z - \mmu_y$:
\begin{align}
\textbf{res:}\qquad &
\mathcal L = \mathrm{NTXent}_{\tau=0.5}(\hat{\mathbf r}) + 5\,
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SIG}}(\mathbf r)
&&\text{(exp-36/49 champion residual objective)},\\
\textbf{res-nplm:}\qquad &
\mathcal L = \mathcal L_{\mathrm{NPLM}}^{\text{inst/bilinear}}(\mathbf r)
+ \mathcal L_{\mathrm{SIG}}(\mathbf r)
&&\text{(exp-59 NPLM residual corner)}.
\end{align}
Constructions per cell: supcon-ft$\to$res, ss-ft$\to$res,
supcon-ft$\to$res-nplm; each evaluated as the residual head space alone
and as the 200-D concat $[\,\text{parent};\text{residual}\,]$.

\section{Training strategies and evaluation}

\paragraph{End-to-end fine-tuning (exp 70).}
Each arm fine-tunes trunk$+$head on the \emph{seen-class} two-view corpus
(held-out classes excluded --- images and labels), unlike earlier
closed-set fine-tunes, so all novelty numbers are strictly open-world.

\paragraph{Discovery (iterated anchor discovery).}
With the fine-tuned trunk frozen as a feature bank and the head as the
discovery backbone: (1)~embed the train pool and pool events beyond the
0.95 distance quantile to the current anchors; (2)~cluster the pool with
BIC-selected $k$-means, creating new anchors (merged at distance $<3$);
(3)~pseudo-label and fine-tune the head with the proto/repulse hybrid
(prototype cross-entropy on $-\tfrac12 d^2$ to anchors $+$ repulsion $+$
SIGReg), balanced batches, 2 rounds $\times$ 5 epochs.  Reported both
``naturally'' (train pool as-is) and on an injected grid (signal fraction
$f$ swept) for post-discovery power.

\paragraph{Metric battery.}
\emph{probe}: AUC of a linear probe trained to separate held-out from
seen test images (3 seeds); \emph{acc}: seen-class nearest-centroid
accuracy; \emph{supAUC}: seen-class supervised separability;
\emph{eucl}/\emph{mahaT}: novelty AUC of min Euclidean/tied-covariance
Mahalanobis distance to seen centroids; \emph{lid}: novelty AUC of the
local-intrinsic-dimension score of Sec.~\ref{sec:lid}; \emph{perevt}:
per-event detection power at $\alpha{=}0.05$ from the distance score;
SparKer/Maha/MMD: dataset-level two-sample test power at injected
fraction $f$ (toy size $n_d{=}1000$--$2000$; SparKer uses an annealed
median-heuristic kernel scale).

\subsection{Local intrinsic dimension as a novelty score (exps 77--79)}
\label{sec:lid}

\paragraph{Definition.}
For a test point $x$, let $r_1 \le \dots \le r_k$ be its distances to
the $k$ nearest \emph{seen-class train} embeddings ($k{=}20$; reference
set subsampled to 4000).  The Levina--Bickel maximum-likelihood
estimator of the local intrinsic dimension is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathrm{LID}}(x) \;=\;
\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{k-1}\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}
\log \tfrac{r_j}{r_k}\Bigr)^{-1},
\label{eq:lid}
\end{equation}
the inversion of the fact that in a locally $d$-dimensional cloud the
neighbor distances scale as $r_j \approx r_k\,(j/k)^{1/d}$.  The novelty
score is $\widehat{\mathrm{LID}}(x)$ itself: higher $=$ more novel.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:lid} sees only distance \emph{ratios}, so it is invariant
to any local rescaling of the space --- it measures the \emph{shape} of
the neighborhood, not its size.  Low dimension leaves neighbors spread
across scales (a same-class twin far closer than the 20th neighbor);
high dimension concentrates all $k$ neighbors onto a thin shell of
nearly equal distances.  A seen-class test point sits \emph{inside} its
own class's compact sheet (low LID); a novel-class point has no
structure of its own in the reference set, so its neighborhood is an
unstructured mixture of class fringes at similar distances (high LID).
Where a distance score asks ``how far am I from the seen data?'', LID
asks ``does the seen data around me look organized, or am I a stranger
standing between structures?''  The estimator needs enough ratios to
average ($k{=}5$ degrades markedly); exact distance ties in collapsed
spaces are capped; per-point values are noisy and only population
separation (AUC) should be interpreted.

\paragraph{Results (exp 78).}
On flowers the unsupervised LID score reaches AUC
$0.951$--$0.955$ in \emph{every} base, exceeding the supervised probe
record (0.906, exp~72) --- the first unsupervised-beats-probe result of
the campaign.  It survives all controls: rotating the holdout on the
frozen trunk (10 random 10-class draws: LID $0.879\pm0.018$ vs
kNN-distance $0.692$; cars under the identical protocol $0.545$),
bootstrap CIs ($\pm0.006$), per-holdout-class minima ($0.86$--$0.92$),
and a $k{=}20$--$50$ plateau.  The edge is regime-specific: novel
flowers sit \emph{on} the flower manifold (near in distance, wrong in
structure), exactly the failure mode of distance scores; on cars and
aircraft, where novelty is off-manifold and distance already separates,
LID adds nothing.

\paragraph{As discovery pool scorer (exp 79).}
Replacing the min-anchor-distance pool threshold with LID in the
discovery loop (\texttt{pool\_score="lid"}) triples round-2 pool purity
on the flowers winners ($0.60$--$0.68$ vs $0.18$--$0.33$): the
discovery fine-tune inflates the space, which silently invalidates the
distance threshold but leaves the scale-free LID untouched.  The
post-discovery probes are nevertheless unchanged ($+0.000$/$-0.029$/
$+0.007$; the 0.906 record ties): above the purity gate, purity is not
the binding constraint --- the discovery fine-tuning dynamics are.

Discovery on the three aircraft res-nplm-concat winners is

\section{Verdicts}

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \textbf{Substrate decides whether discovery helps.}  On frozen
trunks, fine-grained novelty is not geometrically outlying and discovery
erodes the space; on task-fine-tuned trunks the same impure-pool
fine-tune acts as refinement (50/72 cells positive; pool purity can stay
low).  supcon-ft$+$discovery is the only universally safe pipeline
(12/12).
\item \textbf{Champion objectives are regime-determined and
base-invariant.}  Fine-grained many-class $\to$ SupCon$+$SIGReg;
texture $\to$ SimCLR (labels add nothing: DTD record 0.854 is
unsupervised); few-class coarse $\to$ SupCon for the probe and
supervised distance-NPLM for calibration --- the CIFAR class-count rule,
verbatim, on transfer data.
\item \textbf{Residual training $>$ discovery everywhere, and discovery
on top of residuals is purity-gated.}  Residual records: cars 0.855
($+0.088$ over discovery), flowers 0.885, galaxy10 0.975; a further
discovery pass stacks only where the residual space separates the
novelty, adding flowers $\to$ 0.906 and DTD $\to$ 0.862 (exp 72).  The
winning residual objective splits by regime (res-nplm on fine-grained,
plain res on texture/coarse); supcon-ft is the universal parent, and
ss-ft parents fail on fine-grained data because their SIGReg marginal
has already absorbed the residual directions.
\item \textbf{The probe--calibration dissociation is real but not
absolute}: NPLM spaces are the calibrated, distance-ready ones
throughout, softmax spaces the probe-ready ones --- and the
res-nplm concat is the one construction found so far that delivers
both at once.
\item \textbf{The recipe is world-independent} (exps 73--74): on CIFAR
it reproduces every transfer-grid regularity --- concat $>$ parent,
supcon the right parent, the res/res-nplm objective split by regime,
and discovery-on-residuals as purity-gated calibration repair --- and
sets the new CIFAR-100 probe record (0.9594).
\item \textbf{End-to-end fine-tuning erases the frozen-base ranking}
(VISReg, weakest frozen, holds several pre records), extending the
exp-43/49 inversion to the open-world battery.
\end{enumerate}
