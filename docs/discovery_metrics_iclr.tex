\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised objectives with explicit distributional priors --- LeJEPA and
its sketched-isotropic-Gaussian regularizer (SIGReg) being the canonical
example --- promise embedding spaces whose \emph{geometry}, not merely whose
linear separability, carries information. This promise matters most in
scientific settings, where the object of interest is by construction
\emph{unlabeled}: a new particle, a new galaxy morphology, a new material
phase. Yet the field evaluates such spaces almost exclusively with linear
probes, which measure exactly the one property a discovery pipeline cannot
rely on. We argue that a space intended for discovery must be audited in four
distinct currencies --- \emph{detection}, \emph{calibration},
\emph{discoverability}, and \emph{interpretability} --- and we supply a
concrete, reproducible battery for each. Our central result concerns the
discovery of genuinely new classes: under a specified Gaussian marginal, such
a pipeline is not three independent design choices but \textbf{one likelihood
model at three scales}. The Neyman--Pearson density-ratio functional that
shapes the embedding reappears verbatim as the SparKer detection statistic,
and the enforced marginal is precisely what makes Euclidean $k$-means and a
unit-variance BIC correctly specified for proposing the new classes --- so
representation, clustering, and hypothesis test read one quadratic surface. We
characterize when this pipeline finds real classes (a \emph{purity gate}) and
show that below that gate it is a regularizer wearing a discovery costume.
Using a campaign of 79 controlled experiments spanning CIFAR-10/100 and a
$4\times3\times6$ grid of transfer datasets (Stanford Cars, Flowers-102, DTD,
Galaxy10~DECals) $\times$ pretraining bases (DINO, LeJEPA, VISReg) $\times$
contrastive/regularized objectives, we establish four further results. (i) Detection and calibration are
\emph{empirically dissociated}: softmax-contrastive spaces win the probe and
score exactly $0.000$ per-event detection power, while density-ratio-calibrated
spaces invert the ordering; no CIFAR-100 space in our sweep exceeds probe
$0.94$ with tied-Mahalanobis $\auc>0.42$. (ii) The dissociation is
\emph{repairable by construction rather than by loss design}: residual
fine-tuning, which trains a second head on $\z-\mmu_y$ and concatenates, beats
the alternative (iterated anchor discovery) in $12/12$ dataset$\times$base
cells and yields the first spaces that hold a record probe and calibrated
distances simultaneously. (iii) \emph{Scale-free} statistics see novelty that
distance-based scores cannot: local intrinsic dimension (LID) reaches
$\auc=0.951$--$0.955$ on Flowers-102 using \emph{no novelty labels}, exceeding
the best supervised probe ($0.906$) --- the only unsupervised score to beat a
trained probe anywhere in the campaign. (iv) Interpretability is a
\emph{measurable, dissociable} property: superclass agreement and dendrogram
purity track supervision, not probe quality, and a never-labeled held-out class
reliably lands among its true semantic siblings. We distill the results into a
reporting protocol and a set of regime rules --- which objective, which
statistic, which repair --- for practitioners building embedding spaces meant
to find things nobody has labeled.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ==========================================================================

Representation learning is evaluated, overwhelmingly, by how well a linear
classifier reads a representation out. This is a defensible proxy when the
downstream task is classification into known categories. It is the wrong proxy
when the downstream task is \emph{discovery}.

A discovery pipeline in the physical sciences --- searching for a resonance in
collider data, an anomalous transient in a survey, an unfamiliar defect in a
microscopy campaign --- asks a different question of an embedding. It asks:
\emph{given a reference population, is this event unlike anything I have seen,
and by how much?} Answering requires the space to support an absolute notion of
distance, so that a threshold set on simulated background transfers to data. It
requires the tail of the distance distribution to be enriched in genuine
novelty, so that clustering the tail recovers new categories rather than
mislabeled old ones. And --- because a discovery must eventually be
\emph{explained} --- it requires the geometry to be semantically legible, so a
domain expert can say what the new cluster sits next to and why.

None of these are linear separability. All of them are measurable.

\paragraph{The LeJEPA opening}
The recent line of work that regularizes embeddings toward an explicit
distributional target --- most directly LeJEPA and its sketched isotropic
Gaussian regularizer (SIGReg), which penalizes the sliced 1-D Wasserstein-2
distance between the embedding and $\N(0,I)$ --- changes what is available to
measure. Once a marginal is \emph{specified} rather than emergent, distances
have units: a Mahalanobis radius means something, a density ratio can be
calibrated, and a likelihood-based anomaly score becomes well posed. Our
starting hypothesis is that this is the correct substrate for discovery, and
that its evaluation should exploit the structure it provides instead of
discarding it in favor of a probe.

That hypothesis turns out to be half right, in an informative way. A Gaussian
marginal is necessary for calibrated geometry but not sufficient for
detection, and the two properties trade off sharply enough that we treat them
as separate axes throughout.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A four-currency evaluation battery} (\S\ref{sec:battery}) for
embedding spaces intended for discovery: detection, calibration,
discoverability, interpretability --- with concrete estimators, standard
protocols, blind spots, and known failure modes for each.
\item \textbf{Systematic evidence that the currencies dissociate}
(\S\ref{sec:dissoc}), across 79 experiments, two image regimes, four transfer
datasets, three pretraining bases, and ten training objectives. The
dissociation is not noise and is not a tuning artifact: it is reproduced under
paired seeds and inverts predictably with the number of seen classes.
\item \textbf{A unified account of new-class discovery} (\S\ref{sec:discovery}),
which we regard as the central contribution. The Neyman--Pearson functional
minimized during representation learning is, verbatim, the SparKer detection
statistic evaluated at event rather than pair level; and the SIGReg marginal
that licenses the first is what makes the clustering step --- Euclidean
$k$-means with a unit-variance BIC --- correctly specified rather than merely
convenient. We give the \emph{purity gate} that determines when the pipeline
finds real classes, show that below it the loop is a regularizer rather than a
discovery method (established by a reversal in which the outcome flips sign
while purity does not change), and document a bandwidth pathology in kernel
two-sample testing that we expect to affect comparisons beyond this work.
\item \textbf{Two repairs, benchmarked head to head} (\S\ref{sec:repair}):
iterated anchor \emph{discovery} and \emph{residual fine-tuning}. Residual
fine-tuning wins $12/12$ cells and produces the campaign's records.
\item \textbf{Local intrinsic dimension as a first-class novelty statistic}
(\S\ref{sec:lid}), including a full control study: holdout rotation, distance
baselines, bootstrap intervals, per-class decomposition, and $k$-sensitivity.
It sets a new unsupervised record on Flowers-102 and, importantly, we
characterize where it \emph{fails}.
\item \textbf{Interpretability as a metric, not a figure}
(\S\ref{sec:interp}): superclass agreement@$k$, dendrogram purity, centroid
silhouette, and cross-space similarity (CKA, mutual-$k$NN, Procrustes, ridge
$R^2$, TwoNN intrinsic dimension), applied to every space in the campaign.
\end{itemize}

\section{Setup}

\paragraph{Open-world protocol}
In every experiment a set of classes is held out \emph{entirely}: their images
and their labels are removed from the training corpus, so at evaluation time
they are genuinely novel. Unless noted, the held-out set is the final ten
classes of the dataset (Galaxy10: the final one; CIFAR: class 4). Embeddings
are then frozen and scored. This is stricter than the common
``train-on-everything, test-novelty-by-relabeling'' protocol, and we flag the
one legacy table (\S\ref{sec:regimes}, aircraft trunk reuse) where an earlier
run violated it.

\paragraph{Objectives}
All objectives are instances of one design cube, which lets us attribute
effects to axes rather than to named methods. An objective is specified by
four coordinates:
\[
\text{(positives)} \times \text{(critic)} \times \text{(estimator)} \times
\text{(marginal)} .
\]
\emph{Positives} are instance (augmentation) pairs or supervised (same-label)
pairs. The \emph{critic} $g$ is cosine $\langle\hat\z,\hat\z'\rangle/\tau$,
bilinear $\langle\z,\z'\rangle/\tau$, or distance
$-\tfrac12\|\z-\z'\|^2/\tau$. The \emph{estimator} is either the standard
row-normalized softmax (recovering NT-Xent/SupCon) or the un-normalized
maximum-likelihood form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPLM}} \;=\; \E_{\mathrm{ref}}\!\left[e^{g}-1\right]
\;-\; \E_{\mathrm{pos}}\!\left[g\right],
\label{eq:nplm}
\end{equation}
whose minimizer is the \emph{absolute} log density ratio
$g^\star=\log\frac{p(\x,\x')}{p(\x)p(\x')}$ with the calibration constraint
$\E_{\mathrm{ref}}[e^{g^\star}]=1$. The \emph{marginal} is none, global SIGReg,
or classwise SIGReg. The distinction in the estimator axis is the crux of this
paper: softmax self-normalizes per row and therefore recovers pointwise mutual
information only up to an unknown per-row constant, destroying absolute scale;
Eq.~\ref{eq:nplm} does not. We verify the calibration empirically against
analytic PMI on a controlled toy ($r>0.9$).

\paragraph{Two knobs, one inert}
Two hyperparameters govern the NPLM arms, and they are not symmetric. At
initialization the interaction term of Eq.~\ref{eq:nplm} is $\sim\!10^{10}$
while $\lambda\cdot\mathrm{SIGReg}$ is $\sim\!10^{-2}$: \emph{$\lambda$ is
inert for the first $\sim$2 epochs} and only shapes the endgame. By contrast
$\tau$ is a sharp optimum ($\tau=1$ for bilinear on CIFAR-100; $\tau\in
\{0.5,2\}$ each cost $0.06$--$0.10$ probe AUC). We therefore scan $\tau$ and
report $\lambda$ ablations only where the marginal type changes.

\section{A four-currency battery for discovery}
\label{sec:battery}
% ==========================================================================

We propose that any embedding space advertised for discovery be reported in
four currencies. The first two are per-space properties; the third is a
property of a \emph{pipeline}; the fourth is a property of the space's relation
to human categories.

\subsection{Currency I --- Detection}
\emph{Can novelty be read out at all?} Estimator: the AUC of a linear
holdout-vs-rest probe trained on frozen embeddings, reported as mean$\pm$sd
over 3 seeds. This is the standard metric and we retain it, with two caveats
that recur throughout: single-seed spread is $\approx0.017$, so \textbf{gaps
below $0.02$ require 3--5 seeds}; and the probe is blind to radial and
non-linear novelty structure, which is precisely the structure the other
currencies measure.

We also report nearest-centroid top-1 accuracy over seen classes (\texttt{acc})
and the macro one-vs-rest AUC of the prototype posterior (\texttt{supAUC}) as
closed-set sanity checks --- a space can win the novelty probe while being
useless at its nominal task, and we have observed exactly that
(\texttt{nplm-bil-ft} on Flowers: probe $0.808$ post-discovery, \texttt{acc}
$0.531$).

\subsection{Currency II --- Calibration}
\emph{Do distances have absolute meaning?} A discovery threshold is set on a
reference population and applied to data; if the score's scale drifts, the
threshold is worthless even when the space is geometrically fine. We estimate
calibration at two levels.

\emph{Per-space:} novelty AUC of the minimum distance to a seen-class centroid
(\texttt{eucl}), and of tied and per-class Mahalanobis distances
(\texttt{mahaT}, \texttt{mahaPC}; shrinkage $0.1$). Plus a Gaussianity summary
--- eigenvalue conditioning, class RMS, maximum off-diagonal correlation,
sliced-Wasserstein ratio, skew and kurtosis --- which audits whether the SIGReg
target was actually reached rather than merely optimized.

\emph{Per-event:} the fraction of injected signal events exceeding the
$\alpha=0.05$ null quantile of the reference score distribution. This is the
metric that most sharply separates the objective families, and it is the one
closest to how a physics search actually operates. \textbf{SupCon-family spaces
score $0.000$ here}, on every dataset, at every signal fraction: a
self-normalized objective simply does not produce a transferable threshold.

\emph{Dataset-level:} three two-sample batteries --- SparKer (semi-parametric
kernel discrepancy, $M{=}16$ kernels), Mahalanobis, and MMD --- run as toy
experiments ($N_D$ events per toy, signal fraction $f$ injected from the
holdout pool, power $=$ fraction of 50 signal toys above the $\alpha=0.05$
quantile of 200 null toys). Two lessons are protocol-critical. First,
\textbf{kernel statistics must use an annealed median-heuristic bandwidth}: a
fixed $\sigma{=}1$ is calibrated only for the unit-class-width supervised
recipe, and a discovery fine-tune that rescales a compact trunk by $\sim4\times$
sends fixed-$\sigma$ SparKer to zero power at every fraction \emph{while the
space is fine} (annealed: $1.00$). We regard this as a general warning about
kernel two-sample tests across heterogeneous geometries. Second, statistic and
geometry must be matched: parametric statistics need calibrated spaces, and
Mahalanobis is simply dead on CIFAR-100 at every dimension and every space
(inter-class similarity), where MMD remains the dimension-robust choice.

\subsection{Currency III --- Discoverability}
\emph{Does the tail contain the new thing?} A discovery loop pools events past
a high quantile ($0.95$) of the seen-distance distribution, selects a cluster
count by BIC, merges nearby anchors, and fine-tunes the space on seen labels
plus the resulting pseudo-labels. The diagnostic that matters is \textbf{pool
purity}: the fraction of the pooled tail that is genuinely novel. Everything
else about the pipeline is downstream of it.

Purity varies over two orders of magnitude across our datasets --- $0.795$ on
DTD, $0.33$--$0.66$ on Flowers, $0.34$ on CIFAR-10, $0.03$--$0.14$ on Cars,
$0.003$--$0.013$ on CIFAR-100 --- and the variation is \emph{structural}, not a
tuning failure. On CIFAR-100, 500 holdout images sit inside a $\sim$2500-event
tail: the loop is rate-blocked before it begins. On Cars, purity caps at
$0.14$ for a deeper reason we consider one of the campaign's cleanest negative
results: \textbf{a novel car model looks like existing car models}. Pooling
requires novelty that is geometrically \emph{outlying}, and fine-grained
novelty is not, calibrated space or no. We recommend reporting purity
alongside any claimed discovery gain, because a gain at low purity is
mechanistically something else (\S\ref{sec:repair}).

\subsection{Currency IV --- Interpretability}
\emph{Is the geometry legible?} Where a superclass partition exists (CIFAR-100
coarse labels, aircraft manufacturer, car make, galaxy morphology) we compute:
nearest-class-centroid superclass agreement@1 and @5 against chance,
average-linkage dendrogram purity, centroid silhouette, and the
within/between distance ratio. Where it does not, we report top-5
nearest-centroid tables, which are directly auditable by a domain expert.

We additionally characterize how spaces relate to \emph{each other}, on aligned
samples ($n{=}4000$/cell): linear CKA, mutual $k$NN@10, LLE-weight transfer,
orthogonal Procrustes, directional ridge-map $R^2$, calibration transfer
through a fitted ridge map, and TwoNN intrinsic dimension. This turns
``these two objectives learn different things'' from an assertion into a
measurement, and it is how we establish that the NPLM spaces are
\emph{reorganizations} of information the softmax spaces already contain rather
than new information (\S\ref{sec:interp}).

\section{The central dissociation}
\label{sec:dissoc}
% ==========================================================================

The single most robust finding across the campaign is that Currency~I and
Currency~II are \emph{anti-correlated across spaces}. Softmax-contrastive
objectives maximize linearly readable structure and carry no absolute distance
information; density-ratio-calibrated objectives are ready-to-use metric spaces
at a probe cost.

\begin{table}[t]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule

\midrule
\texttt{supcon}                     & \best{0.942} & 0.861 & 0.689 & 0.644 & 0.000 \\
\texttt{supcon\_sigreg}             & 0.882 & 0.844 & 0.637 & 0.577 & 0.000 \\
\texttt{sup} (supervised SIGReg)    & 0.923 & 0.884 & 0.778 & 0.645 & --- \\
\texttt{nplm\_sup\_dist}            & 0.783 & 0.871 & 0.726 & \best{0.723} & \best{0.20} \\
\texttt{nplm\_bilinear} (no labels) & 0.853 & 0.546 & 0.510 & 0.494 & --- \\
\texttt{simclr}                     & 0.867 & 0.729 & 0.236 & 0.229 & 0.000 \\
\texttt{lejepa}                     & 0.864 & 0.695 & 0.408 & 0.371 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The dissociation in one table. The probe ordering and the

\texttt{eucl} of $0.236$ --- \emph{below} chance-adjusted expectations for a

\label{tab:dissoc}
\end{table}

On CIFAR-100 the effect is sharp enough to state as a constraint:
\textbf{no space in the sweep exceeds probe $0.94$ with $\texttt{mahaT}>0.42$},
across 30+ standalone spaces at 32/64/100-D, hub-initialized and from-scratch.
The probe ceiling ($\approx0.95$) is owned by supervised softmax and prototype
recipes; the \texttt{mahaT} ceiling ($\approx0.47$--$0.49$) is owned by NPLM
arms. The frontier between them is populated only by \emph{concatenations}
(\S\ref{sec:repair}).

\paragraph{This is not a seed artifact}
The one place we initially believed the dissociation was breakable ---
\texttt{nplm\_bilinear} reaching probe $0.935$ on CIFAR-100, apparent parity
with \texttt{supcon} --- dissolved under paired multi-seed testing: over 5
paired seeds \texttt{supcon} gives $0.940\pm0.004$ against
\texttt{nplm\_bilinear}'s $0.855\pm0.042$, paired difference $-0.085$
($t=-4.7$). The single-seed $0.935$ was a tail draw from a heavy-tailed seed
distribution. The $10\times$ larger seed variance of the label-free NPLM arm is
itself an unexplained and, we think, interesting phenomenon.

\paragraph{The dissociation recurs at every level}
It is not a property of the loss family alone. It reappears (i) in the
fine-tuning objective: prototype/repulsion fine-tuning wins the probe
($0.97$ vs $0.81$--$0.84$) while NPLM$+$SIGReg fine-tuning wins \emph{every}
power statistic (per-event $0.57$ vs $0.30$--$0.48$; SparKer $1.00/0.92$;
Mahalanobis $0.90$; MMD $0.74$) and holds round-2 pool purity ($0.22$--$0.30$
vs $0.03$--$0.11$); (ii) in the residual construction (\S\ref{sec:repair}),
where NPLM residuals preserve calibration and plain residuals buy probe with
it; and (iii) in semantic structure (\S\ref{sec:interp}), where the
calibration-strong label-free arms are also the semantically emptiest. The
practical rule is uncomfortable but clear: \textbf{choose by consumer}. Linear
readout $\to$ prototype/softmax. Detection with a transferable threshold $\to$
NPLM.

We qualify this in \S\ref{sec:frozen}, where it turns out that a substantial
part of what we are calling a dissociation is a property of the fine-tuning
\emph{update} rather than of the space: on fine-grained data, omitting the
update recovers both currencies at once. The dissociation between
\emph{standalone objectives}, which is what Table~\ref{tab:dissoc} measures,
stands.

\subsection{Regime rules: which corner wins where}
\label{sec:regimes}

The winning objective is determined by regime, and --- importantly for
practitioners --- is \emph{base-invariant}: the same arm wins on DINO, LeJEPA
and VISReg trunks. The governing variable is the number of seen classes
relative to the embedding dimension, modulated by samples/class.

\begin{table}[t]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
regime & example & probe champion & calibration champion \\
\midrule
few classes, coarse       & CIFAR-10, Galaxy10 & \texttt{supcon} & \texttt{nplm\_sup\_dist} \\
many classes, fine-grained & Cars, Flowers, Aircraft & \texttt{ss}/\texttt{supcon\_sigreg} & \texttt{ss} \\
texture                    & DTD & \texttt{simclr} & \texttt{ss} \\
many classes, low-res      & CIFAR-100 & \texttt{supcon} & \texttt{nplm\_bilinear} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Regime rules, replicated on all three pretraining bases. Note the
inversions: the SIGReg marginal \emph{helps} on fine-grained low-data transfer
and \emph{hurts} on CIFAR; label-free instance discrimination wins outright on
texture, where labels add nothing to the probe.}
\label{tab:regimes}
\end{table}

Three sub-rules are worth isolating.

\emph{Anchor realizability gates the classwise marginal.} Classwise SIGReg is
strictly better than global SIGReg \emph{iff} the class anchors are reachable,
i.e.\ $\dim \geq n_{\text{classes}}$. With 100 anchors in 32-D or 64-D,
centroids stall $\approx3.5$ away from their targets and the classwise
marginal becomes actively harmful for label-free arms. This is a geometric
precondition, checkable before training, and we recommend reporting the
centroid-to-anchor distance as a routine diagnostic.

\emph{High dimension is not free for calibrated objectives.} Moving CIFAR-100
from 32-D to 64-D causes the bilinear NPLM arms to \emph{regress on both
currencies} ($0.935\to0.858$; $0.925\to0.825$), while softmax and supervised
distance arms gain mildly. On Cars at 100-D the same signature appears from the
other side: \texttt{nplm\_sup\_dist} gains the most probe ($+0.096$) but loses
its per-event calibration lead ($0.106\to0.03$). At high class counts the extra
dimensions are spent on probe-readable directions, not calibrated ones.

\emph{Fine-tuning inverts the base ranking.} On frozen features DINO dominates
every currency. After end-to-end fine-tuning the ordering largely dissolves ---
VISReg holds several pre-discovery records (DTD \texttt{simclr-ft} $0.854$,
Cars \texttt{ss-ft} $0.741$). Cross-space CKA supplies the mechanism
(\S\ref{sec:interp}): supervision pulls every base toward one shared geometry.

\emph{Cross-entropy is not the ceiling.} On every substrate tested, a
\texttt{supcon\_sigreg} head beats a supervised CE head on every column,
including --- on a LeJEPA fine-tuned trunk --- beating CE's own trained-classifier
top-1 with mere nearest-centroid accuracy ($0.638$ vs $0.625$).

% ==========================================================================
\section{Discovering new classes: one likelihood model, three scales}
\label{sec:discovery}
% ==========================================================================

We now come to what we regard as the central result of this work. The
preceding sections evaluated embedding spaces; this one uses them, and the
finding is that the pieces of an open-world discovery pipeline --- the
representation objective, the clustering that proposes new classes, and the
statistical test that certifies them --- are not three independent design
choices. Under a specified Gaussian marginal they collapse into
\textbf{one likelihood model instantiated at three scales}, and the places
where the pipeline fails are exactly the places where that identification
breaks.

\subsection{The same Neyman--Pearson functional, twice}
\label{sec:np-twice}

The representation objective of \S\ref{sec:battery}, written over pairs, is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}[g]
= \E_{p(x)p(x')}\!\big[e^{g}-1\big] - \E_{p(x,x')}\big[g\big]
\qquad\text{(Eq.~\ref{eq:nplm}, repeated).}
\label{eq:nplm-again}
\end{equation}
The dataset-level detection statistic we use, SparKer
(Grosso et al., \texttt{arXiv:2511.03095}), trains a kernel model $f$ on a
data sample $D$ against an anomaly-free reference $R$ by minimizing
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NP}}[f]
= \sum_{x\in R} w\big(e^{f(x)}-1\big) - \sum_{x\in D} f(x),
\qquad w = N_D/N_R ,
\label{eq:sparker}
\end{equation}
and reports $t_{\mathrm{NP}} = -2\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NP}}$, an asymptotic
log-likelihood-ratio (Wilks) statistic.

These are the same functional. Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparker} is
Eq.~\eqref{eq:nplm} with pairs replaced by events and the product-of-marginals
reference replaced by a control sample. The consequence is not cosmetic:
\textbf{the objective that shapes the embedding is the objective that later
tests it}, so the quantity being estimated during representation learning and
the quantity being thresholded at detection time are the same calibrated log
density ratio. The $e^{f}-1$ term plays an identical role in both --- it pins
$\E_{\mathrm{ref}}[e^{f}]=1$ and thereby fixes the additive constant --- which
is why a softmax-trained space, whose scale is a gauge choice
(App.~\ref{app:grad}), has nothing for a Neyman--Pearson test to threshold and
scores $0.000$ per-event power.

\subsection{Why clustering is correctly specified in a calibrated latent}
\label{sec:cluster-spec}

The third scale is the clustering step, and the same marginal constraint makes
it well posed. Our discovery loop selects the number of new classes by BIC
under \emph{unit-variance} isotropic Gaussians, i.e.\ with per-component log
likelihood $-\tfrac12\|z-\mu_k\|^2 + \text{const}$ and penalty $k\,d\log n$.
That model is ordinarily a crude approximation. Under SIGReg it is not: once
$p(z)=\N(0,I)$ is enforced, Euclidean distance \emph{is} Mahalanobis distance,
so $k$-means is the correct estimator for this likelihood and the BIC is
evaluated under the same density the representation was trained to have. The
clustering, the detection statistic, and the loss are all reading the same
quadratic surface (\S\ref{app:grad}, final subsection).

This is the constructive payoff of the LeJEPA program that motivated the work.
Specifying the marginal is not only a regularizer that improves numbers; it is
what licenses the downstream inference machinery to be applied without
misspecification.

\paragraph{Where the specification is only partial, and what it costs}
Two honest caveats. SIGReg constrains the \emph{aggregate} marginal, not the
per-class conditionals, so ``Euclidean $=$ Mahalanobis'' holds globally but not
necessarily within a discovered cluster; the classwise marginal repairs this
exactly when anchors are realizable (\S\ref{sec:regimes}), which is a
checkable precondition and frequently fails. And the unit-variance BIC has a
documented pathology at calibrated scale: for a cluster of unit width the
$k\,d\log n$ penalty is smaller than the sum-of-squares gain from splitting,
so BIC saturates at $k_{\max}$ and \emph{fragments} a single true class. We do
not regard this as a bug to be tuned away --- it is the correct behaviour of
that likelihood --- and the loop absorbs it downstream with a weighted
union-find merge of anchors closer than a threshold, plus a repulsion
exemption between discovered anchors. Fragmentation is nonetheless the
mechanism behind the worst outcome we record
(\texttt{ss-ft}/LeJEPA on Flowers, \S\ref{sec:purity-gate}).

\subsection{The pipeline}

Concretely, one discovery round is: (i) embed the unlabeled corpus and score
every point by its distance to the nearest seen-class anchor; (ii) \emph{pool}
the points past the $0.95$ quantile of the seen-population score; (iii) choose
$k$ by BIC and cluster the pool; (iv) merge anchors closer than
\texttt{merge\_dist}; (v) assign pseudo-labels and fine-tune the space on seen
labels plus pseudo-labels with a prototype/repulsion objective, co-learning the
new anchors. Two rounds of five epochs is the settled configuration. The
diagnostics recorded per round are the pool size, the \textbf{purity} (fraction
of the pool that is genuinely novel), $\hat k$, the anchor count, the margin
AUC of $d_{\mathrm{seen}}-d_{\mathrm{disc}}$, and the per-holdout anchor AUC.

\subsection{SparKer: what it measures and how it must be run}
\label{sec:sparker}

SparKer models the log density ratio as a sparse self-organizing kernel
mixture, $f(x) = a^{\!\top}[\,p_{[k]}(x)\cdot k(x)\,]$ with Gaussian kernels
$k_i(x)=\exp(-\|x-\mu_i\|^2/2\sigma^2)$ and a gating
$p_i = k_i/\sum_j k_j$; both the $M$ kernel centres $\mu_i$ (initialized from
the data sample) and the weights $a$ are trained on Eq.~\eqref{eq:sparker}. We
use $M=16$ centres and 300 steps. The bandwidth is \emph{annealed} linearly
from $\sigma_0$ to $\sigma_0/10$, with $t_{\mathrm{NP}}$ recorded at three
checkpoints, so a single run probes several resolutions; per-$\sigma$ $p$-values
are calibrated on anomaly-free toys and aggregated as
$-\tfrac12\min_\sigma\log p_\sigma - \tfrac12\,\overline{\log p_\sigma}$, with
detection declared above the $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of the aggregate's own null.
Power is reported with Clopper--Pearson intervals over 50 signal toys against
200 (pre) or 100 (post) null toys at $\alpha=0.05$.

\paragraph{The bandwidth rule, and why it generalizes beyond us}
The single most costly protocol error in the campaign was running SparKer at a
fixed $\sigma=1$. That value is calibrated for the unit class width of the
supervised recipe and \emph{only} for it. A discovery fine-tune rescales a
compact NPLM trunk by roughly $4\times$; at fixed $\sigma$ the test then
reports zero power at every signal fraction \emph{while the space is
perfectly fine} --- the annealed schedule scores $1.00$ on the same
embeddings. We therefore default to the annealed median-heuristic schedule for
any comparison across spaces whose geometry may differ, and we flag the
affected historical rows rather than silently restating them. The general
statement is that a kernel two-sample test carries an implicit length scale,
and comparing such tests across representations \emph{silently compares length
scales unless the bandwidth is set adaptively}. We suspect this affects
published comparisons beyond this work.

\subsection{The residual child is a detector, not just a probe booster}
\label{sec:residual-detector}

An earlier draft of this paper reported the two tables above and noted that the
residual and concatenated spaces of \S\ref{sec:repair} --- which hold every
dataset record here --- had \emph{no} SparKer numbers, because the aircraft
end-to-end cells were run with power disabled and the residual grid never ran
batteries. We have since closed that gap: 106 previously uncovered spaces
across 17 cells, scored under the exp-70 protocol from cached embeddings. The
result changed our reading of the residual construction.

\textbf{The res-nplm residual \emph{child} is the best dataset-level detector in
the fine-grained cells, and it beats its own parent}: aircraft/VISReg
$0.82$ at $f{=}0.05$ against the parent's $0.44$; cars/VISReg $0.96$ and
cars/DINO $0.80$ at $f{=}0.05$ for the child alone. This is a currency in which
we had not previously measured it. \S\ref{sec:repair} presents the residual half
as the component that \emph{retains} calibration while the parent supplies the
probe; the correct statement is stronger --- the calibrated residual half
carries substantial detection power of its own, which the probe and \texttt{eucl}
columns never revealed.

Concatenation again costs nothing: aircraft/VISReg res-cat reaches $1.00$ at
$f{=}0.1$ and galaxy10/VISReg res-nplm-cat $1.00$ at $f{=}0.05$. Taken with
\S\ref{sec:interp}, concatenation is now free in three currencies --- probe,
semantics, and dataset-level power. The base rule of \S\ref{sec:regimes}
reappears here too: LeJEPA-parented residual children are systematically weak
(cars/LeJEPA $0.12$--$0.14$ at $f{=}0.1$ against VISReg's $1.00$), consistent
with LeJEPA parents leaving the most room for residual drift.

\paragraph{Score regime and statistic regime coincide}
The most interesting entry is a negative one. \textbf{Flowers is SparKer-weak in
every residual space} (best $0.94$ at $f{=}0.1$, most $\le0.4$) --- the same
Flowers whose LID novelty score is the strongest unsupervised result in the
campaign (\S\ref{sec:lid}, $0.951$--$0.955$). The on-manifold novelty that a
scale-free per-event statistic detects almost perfectly is nearly invisible to a
dataset-level kernel test. This is not a contradiction but a structural fact
worth stating as a rule: \emph{per-event scale-free detection and dataset-level
kernel detection see different things, and the regimes are close to
complementary}. A practitioner who selects a space on one of them has not
selected it on the other. On CIFAR the ordering reverts to the parents (CIFAR-10
\texttt{supcon} $0.50$ at $f{=}0.01$ and $0.98$ at $f{=}0.02$, with res-nplm-cat
the only construction close at $0.34/0.92$), and CIFAR-100 is flat below
$f{=}0.05$ for every space --- the injection-clamp regime of
\S\ref{sec:purity-gate}, not a property of the geometry.

\paragraph{Statistic--geometry matching}
No single dataset-level statistic dominates. Parametric statistics (per-event
thresholds, Mahalanobis) require the calibrated spaces of
\S\ref{sec:dissoc}; kernel statistics (SparKer, MMD) require scale-matched
bandwidths. Mahalanobis is simply dead on CIFAR-100 at every dimension and
every space, where MMD remains dimension-robust. The practical rule is to
report a battery and match the statistic to the geometry, not to pick a
favourite.

\label{sec:purity-gate}

Pool purity is the make-or-break diagnostic, and it varies by two orders of
magnitude across our datasets for \emph{structural} reasons.

Three conclusions, in increasing order of how much they cost us to learn.

\emph{(1) Pooling requires novelty that is geometrically outlying.} On Cars,
purity caps at $0.14$ because a novel car model looks like existing car
models: the novelty sits \emph{among} the seen classes rather than in the
distance tail, and no amount of calibration moves it there. This is the
cleanest negative result in the campaign and it bounds the applicability of
every tail-pooling discovery method, ours included.

\emph{(2) Above the gate, purity stops being the binding constraint.}
Substituting the scale-free LID score (\S\ref{sec:lid}) for min-anchor
distance as the pool scorer triples round-2 purity --- the distance threshold
decays as the fine-tune inflates the space, collapsing to $0.18$--$0.33$, while
LID holds $0.60$--$0.68$ --- and \emph{strictly dominates} as a pooling
mechanism. The downstream battery does not move ($+0.000$, $-0.029$,
$+0.007$). A mechanism can be strictly better and operationally irrelevant at
the same time; only a multi-currency report reveals both facts.

\emph{(3) At low purity, ``discovery'' is not discovery.} The sharpest
evidence is a reversal. On frozen trunks the loop is probe-negative on Cars for
every arm; on end-to-end fine-tuned trunks it is probe-positive for all six
arms ($+0.03$ to $+0.11$) --- \emph{with purity below $0.14$ in both cases}.
Since purity did not change, the gain cannot come from pure pools. The
impure-pool prototype fine-tune acts as beneficial refinement when the
substrate is aligned to the task and as erosion when it is not. Reported
honestly, the low-purity regime is a regularizer wearing a discovery costume,
and the largest ``gain'' in the program is a rescue of a weak head
(\texttt{nplm-sup-ft} on DTD, $0.586\to0.781$, replicated on all three bases),
not the discovery of anything.

Finally, Aircraft refutes the tempting converse. It pools at adequate purity
($0.32$--$0.53$) and discovery \emph{still} costs probe ($-0.012$ to $-0.037$)
while buying geometry and per-event power (VISReg: per-event $0.30$--$0.50$
across all fractions, mahaT $+0.017$). Purity is necessary, not sufficient;
what fails there is the fine-tune dynamics, not the pool.

the probe, then the erosion should disappear when the space is not updated ---

almost pure cost.

($0.176$--$0.346$ versus $0.023$--$0.065$ unfrozen): a frozen space cannot

remains valid. This is the LID-pooling mechanism of \S\ref{sec:lid} solved from

rescaling. Second, and more consequentially for this paper's framing:
\textbf{on fine-grained cells the probe/calibration dissociation is severable}.
The aircraft record probe $0.8634$ is now retained \emph{with} per-event
$0.523$. The ``choose by consumer'' rule of \S\ref{sec:dissoc} is a statement
about \emph{update dynamics}, not an inherent property of embedding spaces, and
where the update can be omitted the choice does not arise. We state the rule as
the operational recipe: on fine-grained data, run discovery with the space
frozen and discover anchors only.

\subsection{What this means for a discovery pipeline}

Assembling the above: the strongest full pipelines in the campaign are the ones
where all three scales are consistent. On CIFAR-10, an NPLM-calibrated space
with a supervised distance critic plus prototype discovery reaches post-hoc
probe $0.96$--$0.98$ with per-event power $0.73$--$0.75$ against $0.59$ for the
supervised baseline --- the calibrated tail is exactly what pooling needs, the
clustering is correctly specified in that tail, and the same NP functional
certifies the result. Where any one of the three breaks --- rate on CIFAR-100,
tail geometry on Cars, fine-tune dynamics on Aircraft --- the pipeline degrades
in a way the corresponding diagnostic predicts in advance. That predictability,
rather than any single AUC, is what we would want carried forward.

\section{Repairing the dissociation}
\label{sec:repair}
% ==========================================================================

Discovery is one of two constructions that might resolve the dissociation of
\S\ref{sec:dissoc}. Having characterized it above, we benchmark it head to
head against the alternative.

\subsection{Discovery as a repair: substrate-dependent and purity-gated}

Summarizing \S\ref{sec:purity-gate} in the terms of this comparison: across the
full grid the discovery loop is probe-positive in $50/72$ cells, and the
\texttt{supcon-ft}$+$discovery pipeline is positive in $12/12$
dataset$\times$base cells --- the only universally safe full pipeline we found.
Its gains are largest where the pre-discovery space is weakest
($+0.05$ to $+0.15$ on the unsupervised arms) and where purity is high, and it
is flat or negative on fine-grained data regardless of purity.

Two failure modes bound its use as a repair. Flowers/VISReg shows that purity
$0.65+$ can still leave a strong arm flat --- there was nothing left to learn.
And high purity does not protect against a bad \emph{partition}: \texttt{ss-ft}
discovery on the LeJEPA base collapses the probe $0.792\to0.613$, the worst
outcome on record, by splitting round 1 into six anchors at purity $0.605$ that
fragment the strongest classes rather than the novelty --- the BIC
fragmentation of \S\ref{sec:cluster-spec} reaching the probe before
\texttt{merge\_anchors} can absorb it. Treat \texttt{ss-ft}$+$discovery as
unstable and prefer \texttt{supcon-ft} parents when a discovery step is in the
pipeline.

\subsection{Residual fine-tuning wins, and breaks the dissociation}

The alternative construction: freeze the parent's seen-class centroids, train a
second head end-to-end on the residual $\mathbf{r}=\z-\mmu_y$, and evaluate the
concatenation $[\z;\mathbf{r}]$. The parent half keeps what supervision
learned; the residual half is free to encode the within-class variation that
supervision discarded --- which is where fine-grained novelty lives.

\emph{The winning residual objective splits by the same axis as the probe

reappears \emph{inside} the residual construction, which we read as evidence
that it is a property of what the objectives encode rather than of how they are
deployed.

\emph{NPLM residual concatenations keep calibration while raising the probe.}
Cars/VISReg at probe $0.855$ with per-event $0.263$ and the best \texttt{eucl}
and \texttt{mahaT} on Cars to date is \textbf{the first space in the campaign to
hold both currencies at once}. Plain residual concatenations, by contrast,
often buy their probe \emph{with} calibration --- Galaxy10/LeJEPA reaches probe
$0.975$ at per-event $0.001$, which is a warning label, not a win.

\emph{The effect is essentially deterministic.} Multi-seed checks give
Cars/VISReg paired $\Delta=+0.148\pm0.004$ (3/3 seeds) and CIFAR-10 paired
$\Delta=+0.0157\pm0.0004$ (5/5 seeds). We cite records with uncertainties
throughout ($0.833\pm0.017$ on Cars; $0.955\pm0.006$ on CIFAR-100) because
several single-seed peaks in the campaign were fair-but-high draws, and one
objective ranking (plain-res vs res-nplm on CIFAR-100) \emph{flipped} under
averaging.

\emph{Stacking is purity-gated.} Running discovery on top of the residual
winners stacks only where the residual space already separates the novelty:
Flowers $+0.021$ (new record $0.906$) and DTD/VISReg $+0.015$ (new record
$0.862$, finally beating \texttt{simclr-ft}), flat or slightly negative
elsewhere. On fine-grained data the discovery fine-tune erodes exactly the
residual concatenation's probe directions.

% ==========================================================================
\section{Scale-free statistics see what distances miss}
\label{sec:lid}
% ==========================================================================

Every calibration statistic in \S\ref{sec:battery} is a distance. Distances
fail in a specific and predictable way: when novel classes sit \emph{on} the
data manifold, near some seen centroid, minimum-distance scores are blind by
construction. This is the fine-grained regime --- exactly the regime where
pooling fails (\S\ref{sec:repair}).

We therefore add a \emph{scale-free} statistic: the Levina--Bickel estimator of
local intrinsic dimension,
\begin{equation}
\lid_k(\x) \;=\; -\left[\frac{1}{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}
\log\frac{r_j(\x)}{r_k(\x)}\right]^{-1},
\end{equation}
computed against seen-class training references ($k=20$, max 4000 refs). It
depends only on \emph{ratios} of neighbor distances, so it is invariant to the
scale deformations that break both fixed-$\sigma$ kernels and distance
thresholds. Higher $\lid$ indicates novelty: holdout $\lid$ exceeds seen $\lid$
in nearly every space we measured --- novelty lives in locally
higher-dimensional neighborhoods.

\paragraph{Result}
On Flowers-102, $\lid$ reaches $\auc=0.951$--$0.955$ using \emph{no novelty
labels at all}, exceeding the best supervised probe on that dataset ($0.906$).
This is the only unsupervised score to beat a trained probe anywhere in the
campaign, and it is a new Flowers novelty record.

\paragraph{Controls}
We treat a result of this shape with suspicion and ran the corresponding
controls. \emph{Holdout rotation}: over 10 random 10-class holdouts on a frozen
trunk, Flowers $\lid$ gives $0.879\pm0.018$ (min $0.844$) versus a
$k$NN-distance baseline at $0.692\pm0.047$ --- classes 92--101 are not special.
\emph{Distance baselines}: $\lid$ beats $k$NN-distance by $+0.08$--$0.12$ and
\texttt{eucl} by $+0.03$--$0.06$ everywhere on Flowers, with
Spearman$(\lid,k\text{NN-}d)=0.55$--$0.92$ --- correlated, but carrying
independent signal. \emph{Robustness}: bootstrap 95\% CIs $\pm0.006$;
per-holdout-class AUC minimum $0.86$--$0.92$ (no single-class driver);
$k$-sensitivity plateaus at $k\in[20,50]$ and degrades to $\approx0.78$ at
$k=5$ (the ratio estimator needs neighbors). A rank ensemble with \texttt{eucl}
adds nothing --- $\lid$ subsumes it.

\paragraph{And where it fails}
The same protocol on Cars gives $0.545\pm0.040$, and on VISReg fine-tuned Cars
spaces the ordering flips: $k$NN-distance and \texttt{eucl} beat $\lid$.
Aircraft and Galaxy10 sit at $0.56$--$0.66$. \textbf{$\lid$'s advantage is
regime-specific, and the regime is a dataset property, not a protocol
artifact} --- the identical rotation protocol produces $0.88$ on Flowers and
$0.55$ on Cars. The reading: Flowers holdouts sit on the flower manifold with
small distances and mediocre distance-based scores, but a distinct local
dimensional signature; Cars holdouts do not have one.

\paragraph{The signature is class-level, not within-class}
We initially read ``distinct local dimensional signature'' as within-class
structure, and predicted that LID computed on the \emph{residual} half of a
concatenation --- where within-class variation lives by construction --- would
be stronger than on the parent half. It is not. On Flowers the residual half is
uniformly \emph{worse} ($0.923$ vs $0.951$ on DINO, $0.825$ vs $0.893$ on
LeJEPA, $0.733$ vs $0.954$ on VISReg), with the concatenation tracking the
parent. The Flowers LID signal lives in the parent, class-structure half:
\textbf{the novelty is a class-level dimensional anomaly, not a within-class
one}. On Cars the prediction half-holds --- residual-half LID beats parent on
DINO ($0.677$ vs $0.629$) and VISReg ($0.752$ vs $0.682$, the best Cars LID on
record) but not LeJEPA --- and \texttt{eucl} at $0.747$ still matches it, so
there is no regime flip: Cars remains a distance-score dataset.

\paragraph{We could not predict the regime in advance}
The obvious next step is to turn ``which regime am I in'' into a measurement,
since the mechanism above --- holdouts on- versus off-manifold --- looks
label-free. We tried three candidate diagnostics across 17 champion cells (pool
distance-ratio, TwoNN intrinsic-dimension gap between tail and seen population,
and an LID gap) and \textbf{none of them tracks the measured LID$-$\texttt{eucl}
AUC gap}: Spearman $+0.07$, $+0.09$, $-0.16$, with a best sign-accuracy of
$12/17$ against an $11/17$ base rate for always predicting LID. The
LID-versus-distance regime rule therefore remains \emph{empirical}: we can state
which datasets fall on which side and give a mechanism, but we cannot currently
tell a practitioner which side they are on without novelty labels. We report
this because the negative result is more useful than the method we hoped to
deliver.

That same sweep did strengthen LID's standing where it works. LID wins the gap
in $12/17$ champion cells, and on the CIFAR-100 res-nplm concatenation the
margin is decisive --- LID $0.738$ against \texttt{eucl} $0.356$, a $+0.382$ gap
that makes \textbf{LID the only usable novelty score in that space} --- with
galaxy10/LeJEPA at $+0.273$. Aircraft sits firmly on the other side
($-0.11$ to $-0.20$).

\paragraph{$\lid$ as a pool scorer}
Because $\lid$ is scale-free it should also fix the failure mode where a
discovery fine-tune inflates the space and the distance-based pool threshold
decays. It does: substituting $\lid$ for min-anchor-distance as the pool
scorer holds round-2 purity at $0.60$--$0.68$ where the distance scorer
collapses to $0.18$--$0.33$, a $\sim3\times$ improvement, and \emph{strictly
dominates} as a pooling mechanism. But the downstream battery does not move
(probe deltas $+0.000$, $-0.029$, $+0.007$). Above the purity gate, purity is
not the binding constraint --- the fine-tune dynamics are. This is a clean
example of why a battery, rather than a single number, is necessary: a
mechanism can be strictly better and operationally irrelevant at the same time,
and only a multi-currency report shows both facts.

\section{Interpretability, measured}
\label{sec:interp}
% ==========================================================================

A discovery that cannot be explained is not yet a discovery. We treat semantic
legibility as a measurable property and find it dissociates from the other
currencies as sharply as they dissociate from each other.

\paragraph{The founding question}
Are otters near beavers? On a CIFAR-100 supervised parent space with
\texttt{beaver} held out entirely --- never labeled, never in the corpus --- the
nearest-centroid table reads
\texttt{otter}$\to$\texttt{seal}(0.02), \texttt{beaver}$^*$(0.08),
\texttt{bear}(0.11) and
\texttt{beaver}$^*\to$\texttt{otter}(0.08), \texttt{shrew}(0.09),
\texttt{seal}(0.10), \texttt{porcupine}(0.13).
The never-labeled holdout lands mutually top-2 with otter purely from
fine-tuning on the other 99 classes. The same holds on Galaxy10: the
never-labeled \texttt{edge-on bulge} sits $0.06$ from \texttt{edge-on no
bulge}, its true sibling, with \texttt{cigar smooth} next --- which is what an
edge-on disk looks like. Open-world novelty settles where it semantically
belongs, and this is the property that makes a discovered cluster explainable.

\paragraph{Semantics track supervision, not the probe}
The extreme case is Galaxy10 on DINO: \texttt{supcon-ft} recovers the
four-group morphology partition \emph{perfectly} (dendrogram purity $1.000$,
agree@1 $0.9$), while the label-free \texttt{nplm-bil-ft} --- despite its
calibration strengths --- has essentially no semantic structure (purity $0.49$,
silhouette $-0.08$). The probe-versus-calibration dissociation is
\emph{also} a semantic-structure dissociation, and this is the strongest
argument in the paper for reporting Currency~IV separately: a practitioner
selecting on calibration alone can land on a space in which no discovered
cluster can be described.

\paragraph{Concatenation is semantically free}
Residual concatenations inherit the parent's semantics exactly (Galaxy10
res-nplm concat purity $1.000$, equal to parent) while adding the calibration
half. On CIFAR-100 the record residual concatenation \emph{tightens} the
semantic ring around the held-out beaver ($0.06$--$0.10$) at near-parent
purity. Meanwhile the residual half \emph{alone} scrambles semantics by design
(beaver $\to$ mushroom/crab/lobster, purity $0.365$) --- semantics ride in the
parent half, and the concatenation restores them while keeping the probe gain.
This is a strong practical argument for concatenation over replacement.

\paragraph{What ``fine-grained mistakes'' actually are}
On Cars, the space organizes by body style as much as by make (make-agree@1
$0.40$ against chance $0.04$), with errors like Audi convertible $\to$ BMW
convertible. These are not failures; they are visually coherent groupings that
a make-based partition scores as wrong. We note this because superclass
agreement metrics can penalize exactly the structure a scientist would want.

\subsection{How spaces relate to each other}

Cross-space measurements supply mechanisms for several claims above.

\emph{Supervision erases the pretraining base; self-supervision preserves it.}
The same supervised arm fine-tuned from two \emph{different} bases reaches
CKA $\approx0.74$ on average --- as similar as two \emph{different supervised
losses} on the same base. The label signal pulls every pretraining toward one
shared supervised geometry, which is precisely why the regime rules of
\S\ref{sec:regimes} are base-invariant. Label-free \texttt{simclr-ft} is far
less base-invariant (CKA $0.46$--$0.62$). Consistently, on DTD --- where
\texttt{simclr} wins the probe --- \texttt{simclr-ft}'s \emph{local} structure
transfers across bases better than the supervised arms' (mutual-$k$NN $0.28$ vs
$0.16$): its texture neighborhoods are the transferable part.

\emph{NPLM spaces are reorganizations, not new information.} The NPLM arms are
geometric outliers everywhere (CKA $0.2$--$0.36$, mutual-$k$NN $0.03$--$0.06$
against every other arm, LLE weight-transfer ratio $>1$) --- yet they are
highly \emph{decodable from} softmax spaces (asymmetric ridge $R^2$ up to
$0.97$ at CKA $0.36$). Calibration is a re-encoding of information the
supervised space already contains. This explains why concatenation works so
well and why a single loss struggles: the two currencies are different
\emph{codes} for overlapping content.

\emph{Residual children are mostly linear re-maps --- and drift is capacity,
not help.} Parent-vs-child ridge $R^2$ is $0.97$--$0.98$ on coarse data
(the child is nearly a linear re-map; calibration is re-weighting) and
$0.91$--$0.95$ with mutual-$k$NN $\approx0.2$ on fine-grained data (locally
reorganized, $5$--$10\%$ genuinely new variance). Within the transfer grid the
concatenation gains track the unexplained fraction. But CIFAR refutes the naive
version of this rule: there children drift \emph{much} further ($R^2$ down to
$0.32$) while producing the campaign's \emph{smallest} probe gains
($+0.006$--$0.016$ vs Cars' $+0.148$). Unexplained variance is necessary and not
sufficient --- what pays is whether the new variance encodes the holdout.

\emph{Loss families have intrinsic-dimension fingerprints.} TwoNN intrinsic
dimension orders the arms identically in every cell: NPLM supervised most
compressed (ID $\approx2$--$3$), SIGReg/SSL arms mid ($5$--$7$), softmax and
frozen highest ($9$--$13$). The Gaussian target and the density-ratio collapse
are directly visible as dimension, which makes ID a cheap objective-provenance
diagnostic.

\section{A reporting protocol}
% ==========================================================================

We distill the campaign into a protocol we would want applied to any embedding
space advertised for scientific discovery.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Report all four currencies.} Probe-only or geometry-only
conclusions are, in our experience, wrong roughly as often as they are right,
because the currencies are anti-correlated by construction.
\item \textbf{State the number of seen classes, the embedding dimension, and
the centroid-to-anchor distance.} These determine which corner of the design
cube is even available (classwise marginals require
$\dim \geq n_{\text{classes}}$).
\item \textbf{Never claim a probe gap below $0.02$ from one seed.} Single-seed
spread is $\approx0.017$. Two rankings in this campaign flipped under
averaging.
\item \textbf{Use annealed median-heuristic bandwidths} for any kernel
two-sample statistic compared across spaces with different scales. A fixed
bandwidth reports zero power on a space that is fine.
\item \textbf{Report pool purity next to any discovery gain.} A gain at purity
$<0.15$ is refinement, not discovery, and behaves differently (it depends on
substrate alignment, not on the pool).
\item \textbf{Include at least one scale-free statistic, and do not assume it
agrees with your dataset-level test.} Distance scores are structurally blind to
on-manifold novelty; $\lid$ costs nothing and, in one regime, beat every
supervised alternative we had. But the per-event and kernel-test regimes are
close to complementary (\S\ref{sec:residual-detector}): Flowers is the
campaign's best LID dataset and among its worst SparKer datasets. Report both;
neither substitutes for the other. We cannot currently tell you in advance
which regime you are in (\S\ref{sec:lid}).
\item \textbf{Before fine-tuning as part of discovery, check whether you need
to.} On fine-grained data the discovery gain lives in the anchors and the
update is close to pure cost (\S\ref{sec:frozen}); freezing the space makes the
step strictly non-negative and keeps the pool threshold valid into round two.
\item \textbf{Audit semantics explicitly.} Superclass agreement, dendrogram
purity, and a nearest-centroid table a domain expert can read. Calibration
does not imply legibility --- we have a space with strong calibration and
silhouette $-0.08$.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{And a default recipe}
For a practitioner who wants one starting point: fine-tune end-to-end with
\texttt{supcon} (adding a global SIGReg marginal if the data are fine-grained
and low-shot), then train a residual head on $\z-\mmu_y$ with an NPLM
objective on fine-grained data or a softmax objective on coarse/texture data,
and evaluate the concatenation. Add a discovery pass only if measured pool
purity exceeds $\approx0.3$. Score with the four-currency battery, including
$\lid$.

% ==========================================================================
\section{Limitations}
% ==========================================================================

Our evidence is entirely in the image domain; the regime rules are stated in
terms of class counts and samples per class and we expect them to transfer, but
we have not tested them on the scientific modalities (event-level collider
data, spectra, time series) that motivate the work. The ``discovery'' setting
is simulated by holding out known classes, which is a proxy: real novelty is
not a withheld member of a curated taxonomy, and the Cars result --- where
novel classes are not geometrically outlying --- suggests the proxy's
difficulty varies more than the datasets' nominal difficulty does. Several
conclusions rest on 3--5 seeds, which resolves $0.02$-scale effects but not
smaller ones. Two claims in the paper rest on parametrizations we did
not fully test: the calibration identity does not hold for the bias-free
distance critic our implementation uses (App.~\ref{app:grad}), so the
distance-NPLM arms' detection strength is evidence of good scaling rather than
of achieved calibration, and adding the bias term is untested. And we cannot
predict the LID-versus-distance regime without labels
(\S\ref{sec:lid}) --- three candidate diagnostics failed --- so that rule is a
description of our datasets, not a tool. Our discovery loop is one specific algorithm (quantile pool, BIC
$k$-selection, anchor merge, prototype fine-tune) and the purity-gating
conclusion may be specific to it. Finally, the interpretability metrics require
a superclass partition, which is available in our datasets and frequently is
not in the settings we care about; the nearest-centroid tables remain
available but require expert reading rather than a number.

% ==========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% ==========================================================================

Embedding spaces built for scientific discovery need to be evaluated as
instruments, not as classifiers. When we do so --- across 79 controlled
experiments, seven datasets, three pretraining bases and ten objectives --- the
picture is consistent. The part we would most want carried forward is the
unification of \S\ref{sec:discovery}: a pipeline that discovers new classes
need not bolt a clustering heuristic and an anomaly test onto a representation
trained for something else. Fix the marginal, and the density-ratio objective
that shapes the space is the statistic that certifies a discovery, while the
clustering that proposes candidates is correctly specified under the same
density. When the pipeline then fails, it fails for a reason the
diagnostics name in advance --- rate on CIFAR-100, tail geometry on Cars,
fine-tune dynamics on Aircraft --- which we value more than any single AUC.
Alongside that: linear readability and calibrated geometry are
anti-correlated properties that no single loss in our design space achieves
together; the repair is architectural (residual decomposition and
concatenation) rather than a better loss; scale-free statistics reach novelty
that distance-based scores cannot see, in a regime one can identify in advance;
and semantic legibility is a third, separately measurable axis that tracks
supervision alone. The distributional-prior program that LeJEPA and SIGReg
opened up is, we think, the right foundation --- specifying the marginal is
what makes any of these quantities well posed. What it needs alongside it is a
metrology: a standard battery, honest uncertainties, and the discipline to
report the currency a space \emph{lacks}.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility statement}

All experiments are scripted (\texttt{experiments/NN\_*.py}, each with a
mandatory \texttt{--quick} smoke mode) over a shared library
(\texttt{supersig/}: losses, training loops, metrics, discovery). Result arrays
are archived as \texttt{logs/expNN/*.npz} with fixed key conventions, condensed
into per-dataset master tables under \texttt{docs/}. Seeds are fixed and
identical across paired cells; the probe uses seeds $1000{+}s$. Known
reproduction jitter from cuDNN nondeterminism is $\approx\pm0.003$ in AUC and is
stated wherever single-seed records are cited.

\section{Gradient descent under NPLM versus InfoNCE, and the log-likelihood
readout}
\label{app:grad}
% ==========================================================================

The dissociation of \S\ref{sec:dissoc} is stated empirically in the main text.
This appendix derives it. We compare the two estimators of the same population
object --- the pointwise mutual information --- at the level of their
\emph{gradients}, and show that essentially every qualitative difference
reported in the campaign (the vanishing per-event power of softmax spaces, the
inertness of $\lambda$, the heavy-tailed seed variance of the bilinear NPLM
arm, the base-robustness of the distance NPLM arm, and the intrinsic-dimension
fingerprints) follows from a single structural fact: \textbf{InfoNCE is flat
along the direction that carries the absolute normalization, and NPLM is
strictly convex along it.}

We flag scope honestly. The derivations in
\S\ref{app:rowsum}--\S\ref{app:forces} are analytical and new to this paper;
the links drawn to campaign measurements in \S\ref{app:predictions} are
\emph{post-hoc consistency checks} against data already collected, not
preregistered tests. We indicate in each case what a decisive experiment would
be.

\subsection{Two estimators, one target}

Fix a batch $\{(x_i,x_i^+)\}_{i=1}^N$ and write $g_{ij}=g_\theta(x_i,x_j^+)$,
with the diagonal as positive pairs and the off-diagonal as the shuffled
product-of-marginals reference. The two objectives are
\begin{align}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NCE}}
&= -\frac1N\sum_i \Big[\,g_{ii} - \log\!\sum_j e^{g_{ij}}\Big],
\label{eq:app-nce}\\
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}
&= \frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{i\neq j}\big(e^{g_{ij}}-1\big)
- \frac1N\sum_i g_{ii}.
\label{eq:app-nplm}
\end{align}
They share the batch geometry exactly; only the functional differs. Both are
consistent estimators of $\mathrm{PMI}$ --- but of different \emph{gauge
classes} of it, as the gradients make immediate.

\subsection{The row-sum identity: a flat direction versus a convex one}
\label{app:rowsum}

Differentiating \eqref{eq:app-nce} with respect to the critic entries gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NCE}}}{\partial g_{ij}}
= \frac1N\big(\sigma_{ij} - \delta_{ij}\big),
\qquad
\sigma_{ij} = \frac{e^{g_{ij}}}{\sum_k e^{g_{ik}}},
\label{eq:app-gnce}
\end{equation}
while \eqref{eq:app-nplm} gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}}{\partial g_{ij}}
=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{e^{g_{ij}}}{N(N-1)}, & i\neq j\ \ (\text{reference}),\\[6pt]
-\dfrac1N, & i=j\ \ (\text{positive}).
\end{cases}
\label{eq:app-gnplm}
\end{equation}
Three differences are visible at once.

\paragraph{(i) InfoNCE gradients are row-stochastic; NPLM's are not}
From \eqref{eq:app-gnce}, $\sum_j \partial\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NCE}}
/\partial g_{ij} = 0$ for \emph{every} anchor $i$, because $\sigma_{i\cdot}$ is
a probability vector. The InfoNCE gradient is therefore purely
\emph{relative}: it can reallocate mass among the entries of a row but can
never change a row's total. Formally, consider shifting the critic by an
arbitrary per-anchor function, $g_{ij}\mapsto g_{ij}+c_i$. Then
$\sigma_{ij}$ is unchanged and
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NCE}}[g+c] = \widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NCE}}[g]
\quad\text{exactly, for all } c .
\end{equation}
The per-anchor shift is an exact \emph{flat direction} of the loss --- zero
gradient, zero curvature. This is the entire content of ``InfoNCE recovers PMI
up to a per-row constant'': the constant is not merely unidentified, it is
\emph{invisible to gradient descent}, so no amount of training, data, or
capacity can recover it.

For NPLM the same shift gives, with $\bar{c}$ a uniform shift for clarity,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}[g+\bar c]
= e^{\bar c}\,\widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{ref}}\big[e^{g}\big] - 1
- \widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{pos}}[g] - \bar c,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
\frac{d\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}}{d\bar c}
= e^{\bar c}\,\widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{ref}}\big[e^{g}\big] - 1,
\qquad
\frac{d^2\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}}{d\bar c^{\,2}}
= e^{\bar c}\,\widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{ref}}\big[e^{g}\big] \;>\; 0 .
\label{eq:app-convex}
\end{equation}
The direction InfoNCE cannot see is \emph{strictly convex} for NPLM, with a
unique minimum at $\bar c^\star = -\log\widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{ref}}[e^{g}]$,
i.e.\ exactly at the calibration condition
$\widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{ref}}[e^{g^\star}]=1$. Equivalently: summing
\eqref{eq:app-gnplm} over all pairs,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}}{\partial g_{ij}}
= \widehat{\E}_{\mathrm{ref}}\big[e^{g}\big] - 1
= \text{(calibration residual)} .
\label{eq:app-residual}
\end{equation}
\textbf{The total gradient of the NPLM loss \emph{is} the miscalibration.}
Gradient descent on \eqref{eq:app-nplm} performs calibration as an inseparable
part of fitting, and \eqref{eq:app-residual} is computable at zero cost during
training.

We flag one negative result about how far that observation goes. It is
tempting to use the residual as a \emph{label-free model-selection} criterion
--- prefer the seed whose terminal residual is closest to zero --- and we
proposed exactly that. It does not work: over five paired seeds the rank
correlation between $|$residual$|$ and probe is $+0.30$ and $-0.10$ on the two
bilinear arms, and selecting the best-of-five by residual \emph{underperforms}
the random-seed mean ($0.839$ against $0.891$). Part of the reason is
structural --- for distance critics the residual cannot reach zero at all, see
below --- but even for bilinear critics, where it can, per-seed variation in
calibration does not track per-seed variation in probe quality. The seed
variance appears to live in the balance between the interaction and marginal
terms rather than in calibration failure. Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-residual} remains a
valid \emph{diagnostic} of whether a run calibrated; it is not a predictor of
how good the resulting space is.

This is the precise sense in which per-event detection power is
\emph{structurally} unavailable to a softmax objective. A per-event threshold
is a statement about the absolute value of the score; \eqref{eq:app-gnce} says
that value is a gauge choice on which the objective exerts no force. The
observed $0.000$ per-event power of every SupCon-family space in the campaign
is not a tuning failure or a weak baseline --- it is this identity.

\paragraph{(ii) Why max-subtraction destroys calibration}
The standard numerical guard for softmax losses, $g_{ij}\mapsto
g_{ij}-\max_k g_{ik}$, is a per-anchor shift. By (i) it is exactly free for
InfoNCE and exactly \emph{fatal} for NPLM: it moves the minimizer from
$g^\star$ to $g^\star + c$, the one quantity the loss was chosen to pin. The
correct guard clamps the exponent, $e^{\min(g,\,g_{\max})}$, which by
\eqref{eq:app-gnplm} sets the gradient of pathological reference pairs to a
constant instead of shifting the optimum, leaving $g^\star$ untouched wherever
$g<g_{\max}$. The campaign uses $g_{\max}=30$ and verifies the resulting critic
against analytic PMI at correlation $>0.9$ with mean offset $\approx0$;
max-subtraction drives the offset to hundreds.

\paragraph{(iii) Bounded versus exponential gradients}
From \eqref{eq:app-gnce}, $|\partial\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NCE}}/
\partial g_{ij}|\le 1/N$ \emph{unconditionally}. InfoNCE cannot produce a large
critic gradient no matter how badly scaled the embedding is. From
\eqref{eq:app-gnplm}, the NPLM reference gradient is $e^{g_{ij}}/N(N-1)$, which
is unbounded above. This single asymmetry accounts for the optimization
folklore around NPLM, and we can quantify it.

\subsection{Gradient variance and the two critics}
\label{app:variance}

Suppose the critic values on reference pairs are approximately Gaussian,
$g \sim \N(\mu,s^2)$. Then the relative variance of the reference gradient is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{Var}\big[e^{g}\big]}{\big(\E[e^{g}]\big)^2}
= e^{s^{2}} - 1 ,
\label{eq:app-var}
\end{equation}
which grows \emph{exponentially} in the spread of the critic, while the
corresponding quantity for \eqref{eq:app-gnce} is $O(1)$ because $\sigma_{ij}
\in[0,1]$. A modest critic spread of $s=3$ already gives a relative gradient
standard deviation of $\approx90$. NPLM training is therefore not merely
``sometimes unstable'': its gradient noise is controlled by a quantity, $s$,
that is itself a free consequence of the parametrization.

This makes the \emph{critic} axis of the design cube, which in the main text is
one knob among four, the decisive one for optimization. Compare the two critics
used in the campaign:
\begin{align}
\text{distance:}\quad & g = -\tfrac12\|z-z'\|^2/\tau \;\le\; 0
&&\Longrightarrow&& e^{g}\le 1 \quad\text{(bounded by construction)},\\
\text{bilinear:}\quad & g = \langle z,z'\rangle/\tau \;\in\;\R
&&\Longrightarrow&& e^{g}\ \text{unbounded}.
\end{align}
The distance critic makes the exponential gradient \emph{automatically bounded}
--- the pathology of \eqref{eq:app-var} cannot occur, and the clamp never
activates in the normal range. The bilinear critic has no such protection: a
single well-aligned reference pair can dominate the batch gradient.

\paragraph{The same bound makes a bare distance critic uncalibratable}
The boundedness is not free, and we initially missed what it costs. If
$g\le 0$ everywhere then $e^{g}\le 1$, hence
\begin{equation}
\E_{\mathrm{ref}}\big[e^{g}\big] \;\le\; 1
\qquad\text{with equality only in the degenerate case } g\equiv 0 ,
\end{equation}
so the calibration residual of Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-residual} is
\emph{structurally negative} and the condition $\E_{\mathrm{ref}}[e^{g^\star}]=1$
is \textbf{unreachable}. A bare distance critic cannot calibrate, however well
it is optimized. The resolution is the bias term of the full parametrization,
$g = -\tfrac12\|z-z'\|^2 + b(x) + b(x')$, which restores the positive range;
the implementation used throughout this campaign omits it. We therefore
qualify the claim made in \S\ref{sec:np-twice}: the $e^{g}-1$ term pins the
absolute normalization \emph{for the unconstrained functional and for critic
families that can represent positive values} --- the bilinear critic, or
distance-plus-bias --- but not for the bias-free distance critic as
implemented. The distance-NPLM arms' strong per-event and kernel-test power
(\S\ref{sec:sparker-results}) is therefore evidence that their geometry is
well-scaled, not that they achieve the calibration identity. Adding the bias
and re-testing is the obvious experiment and we have not run it.

\subsection{The force law on the embedding}
\label{app:forces}

Push the gradients through to the embedding itself. Under the distance critic
(the form the critic collapses to once SIGReg is active), $\partial g_{ij}/
\partial z_i = -(z_i - z_j^+)$, and
\begin{equation}
\nabla_{z_i}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{NPLM}}
= \underbrace{\frac1N\big(z_i - z_i^{+}\big)}_{\text{spring to positive}}
\;-\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\sum_{j\neq i}
e^{-\frac12\|z_i-z_j^{+}\|^{2}}\big(z_i - z_j^{+}\big)}_{\text{repulsion}} .
\label{eq:app-force}
\end{equation}
Two features of \eqref{eq:app-force} are worth stating plainly.

\emph{The repulsion is the gradient of a kernel density estimate.} The weight
$e^{-\frac12\|z_i-z_j\|^2}$ is an unnormalized isotropic Gaussian kernel, so
the second term is precisely $\nabla_{z_i}\hat q(z_i)$ for
$\hat q$ a Gaussian KDE of the reference population. NPLM gradient descent is
therefore: \emph{pull toward your positive with a linear spring, and descend the
estimated reference density}. The unit-width Gaussian supplies an intrinsic
\textbf{length scale}, and that length scale is what makes distances absolute.
A point far from all references feels essentially \emph{no} repulsion --- the
force vanishes as $e^{-d^2/2}$ --- so the space does not expend capacity
separating what is already separated.

\emph{InfoNCE has no length scale.} The corresponding repulsion weight is
$\sigma_{ij}$, which is normalized \emph{within a row}: even if every negative
is far away, the row still sums to one and the repulsion is redistributed
rather than extinguished. Contrastive repulsion is thus long-range and
rank-based, and its fixed point is uniformity on the sphere rather than any
particular scale. This is the geometric statement behind the main text's
observation that \texttt{simclr} attains probe $0.867$ with \texttt{eucl}
$0.236$: the objective arranges \emph{angles} correctly and never had an
opinion about radii.

\emph{The positive term does not saturate.} In \eqref{eq:app-gnce} the positive
gradient is $\tfrac1N(\sigma_{ii}-1)$, which $\to 0$ as the positive is
separated: InfoNCE anneals itself. In \eqref{eq:app-gnplm} it is the constant
$-\tfrac1N$. NPLM pulls positives together with undiminished force forever, and
equilibrium is reached \emph{only} when the exponential repulsion balances it
--- that is, at the calibration condition, not at a separation criterion.

\subsection{What this predicts, and what the campaign measured}
\label{app:predictions}

The derivation yields five predictions. All five have a counterpart in the
campaign data. We stress again that these are consistency checks on existing
measurements, not independent confirmations.

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep3pt
\item \emph{Softmax spaces have exactly zero transferable per-event power,
regardless of probe quality.} Predicted by the flat direction in (i).
Measured: per-event $0.000$ for every SupCon-family space on every dataset and
every signal fraction, including spaces at probe $0.94$.

\item \emph{$\lambda$ is inert early; $\tau$ is not.} At initialization with a
bilinear critic on raw (unnormalized) embeddings, $g$ has spread of order tens,
so by \eqref{eq:app-gnplm} the interaction gradient is of order $e^{g}$ ---
numerically $\sim\!10^{10}$ against $\sim\!10^{-2}$ for
$\lambda\cdot\mathrm{SIGReg}$. The marginal term cannot compete until the
exponent has contracted, which takes $\approx2$ epochs; $\lambda$ then shapes
only the endgame. By contrast $\tau$ rescales the exponent itself and hence
enters the gradient multiplicatively at all times. Measured: $\lambda$
ablations are flat, $\tau=1$ is a sharp optimum on CIFAR-100 with $\tau\in
\{0.5,2\}$ each costing $0.06$--$0.10$ probe.

\item \emph{The bilinear NPLM arm should be the high-variance one; the distance
NPLM arm should be the stable one.} Predicted by \S\ref{app:variance}:
$e^{g}\le1$ automatically for the distance critic, unbounded for bilinear.
Measured, and to our mind the appendix's most striking correspondence:
\texttt{nplm\_bilinear} on CIFAR-100 gives $0.855\pm0.042$ over paired seeds,
a spread $10\times$ that of \texttt{supcon} ($0.940\pm0.004$), with a heavy
right tail that produced a single-seed $0.935$ we initially mistook for parity;
whereas \texttt{nplm\_sup\_dist} is \emph{the most base-robust arm in the
campaign}, probe $0.72$--$0.73$ across all three pretraining trunks (spread
$0.009$, versus \texttt{supcon}'s $0.076$). The unexplained seed variance
flagged in \S\ref{sec:dissoc} is, on this account, the exponential gradient
variance of \eqref{eq:app-var} --- and the prediction is that it is a property
of the \emph{critic}, not of the estimator.

\emph{We have since run the decisive test} --- estimator held at NPLM, critic
swept against positives at matched $\tau$, five paired seeds --- and it
confirms the attribution while correcting our proposed diagnostic. Probe
standard deviation splits $3.1\times$ by critic (bilinear $0.055$, distance
$0.018$) against only $1.6\times$ by positives, and \texttt{dist-sup} is the
stable corner at $0.8366\pm0.0125$. But the quantity we suggested logging,
the raw critic spread $s=\mathrm{sd}(g)$, \emph{anti}-correlates with the probe
spread ($-0.80$), while the spread of $e^{g}$ correlates $+0.80$ and separates
the arms by two orders of magnitude ($0.1$--$0.3$ for distance critics,
$12$--$35$ for bilinear). The reason is the one-sidedness noted above:
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-var} assumed $g$ approximately Gaussian, and a distance
critic is bounded above, so it can carry a large $\mathrm{sd}(g)$ over
strongly negative values while $e^{g}$ stays squashed near zero. \textbf{The
theory holds in its exponential form only}; practitioners should monitor
$\mathrm{sd}(e^{g})$, not $\mathrm{sd}(g)$.

\item \emph{NPLM spaces should be compressed relative to softmax spaces.}
Predicted by the short-range force law: distant pairs exert no force, and the
non-saturating positive pull is balanced only at the calibration scale.
Measured: TwoNN intrinsic dimension orders the arms identically in every cell of
the grid --- supervised NPLM $\approx2$--$3$, SIGReg/SSL $5$--$7$, softmax and
frozen $9$--$13$.

\item \emph{NPLM should reorganize rather than add information.} The Bregman
identity for \eqref{eq:app-nplm} states that the constrained minimizer is the
projection of $\mathrm{PMI}$ onto the critic family in the divergence generated
by $\phi(t)=e^{t}$; a different projection of the same target is still a
function of that target. Measured: NPLM spaces are geometric outliers against
every other arm (CKA $0.2$--$0.36$, mutual-$k$NN $0.03$--$0.06$) yet are highly
\emph{decodable from} softmax spaces (ridge $R^2$ up to $0.97$ at CKA $0.36$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the log-likelihood readout needs both terms}

Finally, the gradient view sharpens why neither objective alone delivers an
interpretable likelihood surface, which is the motivation for the hybrid used
throughout the campaign.

NPLM fixes the interaction and its absolute scale, but the critic family
$g = \langle z,z'\rangle + b(x) + b(x')$ retains a gauge,
\begin{equation}
z \mapsto Rz + a, \qquad
b(x) \mapsto b(x) - \langle a, z\rangle - \tfrac12\|a\|^2 + \beta ,
\end{equation}
under which $g$, and hence the loss and \emph{every gradient}, is exactly
invariant. So NPLM alone determines what the pair interaction is but not how it
is split between the embedding and the bias --- the map into ``log-likelihood
space'' is undetermined by descent. SIGReg removes precisely this residual
freedom: pinning $p(z)=\N(0,I)$ kills the shift $a$ (leaving only an irrelevant
rotation $R$) and identifies the bias as the marginal log-density,
$b_\theta(x)\to-\tfrac12\|z\|^2+\text{const}$. Substituting back collapses the
critic to the pure distance form and yields the three quadratic identities
\begin{equation}
\log p(x) = -\tfrac12\|z\|^2 + c_0,
\qquad
\log\frac{p(x,x')}{p(x)p(x')} = \langle z,z'\rangle,
\qquad
\log p(x,x') = -\tfrac12\|z-z'\|^2 + c_1 .
\end{equation}
The division of labour is exact and is a statement about descent directions:
\textbf{NPLM supplies curvature along the normalization direction that InfoNCE
leaves flat; SIGReg supplies curvature along the gauge directions that NPLM
leaves flat.} Each term is the other's null space.

Two caveats bound the claim, and both are visible in the main text's results.
The identities hold exactly only when $\mathrm{PMI}$ lies in the
bilinear-plus-separable family; otherwise the Bregman projection returns the
nearest such surface, and the residual is real --- it is one reading of why
concatenating a softmax half with an NPLM half outperforms either alone
(\S\ref{sec:repair}), since the two halves are different projections of
overlapping content. And the readout degrades under collapse: at small
embedding dimension and small $\tau$ the supervised optimum is a simplex
equiangular tight frame in which all class centres are equidistant and the
likelihood information is destroyed. The campaign's realizability condition
($\dim \ge n_{\text{classes}}$ for classwise anchors, \S\ref{sec:regimes}) is
the practical form of that caveat, and the observed regression of the bilinear
NPLM arms at 64-D on CIFAR-100 --- losing \emph{both} currencies --- is the
kind of failure it predicts.

\section{Discovery reach: the fraction at which SparKer power crosses 0.95}
\label{app:reach}
% ==========================================================================

Power reported at a fixed signal fraction answers ``how well does this space do
on my grid''; a search wants the inverse quantity --- \emph{how small a signal
can this space find?} We therefore invert every archived power curve into a
single sensitivity number,
\begin{equation}
f_{95} \;=\; \min\;\{\, f \;:\; \mathrm{power}_{\mathrm{SparKer}}(f)\ \ge\ 0.95 \,\},
\end{equation}
the injected fraction at which the test becomes reliable. Lower is better. We
take the first upward crossing and interpolate linearly in $\log f$, power
curves being sigmoidal in $\log f$.

\paragraph{The measurement is limited by the fraction grid, not by the spaces}
This is the first thing to report, and it governs how the table should be read.
Power is estimated from 50 signal toys, so it is quantized in steps of $0.02$
and carries a Clopper--Pearson uncertainty of roughly $\pm0.03$ near $0.95$.
More seriously, the transfer grid is
$f\in\{0.003,0.01,0.02,0.05,0.1\}$, and essentially every crossing falls in the
final interval $[0.05,0.1]$, across which power typically rises from $\sim0.1$
to $\sim1.0$. Of 106 newly covered spaces, 46 cross $0.95$ inside the grid at
all, and \textbf{only four have a crossing that is not pinned to that top
interval}. We mark the rest with $*$ and treat them as ``somewhere in
$(0.05,0.1)$'' rather than as measurements. The cleanly determined values are
CIFAR-10 \texttt{supcon} ($f_{95}=0.019$) and \texttt{res-nplm} concat
($0.023$), Galaxy10/VISReg \texttt{supcon-ft-resnplm-cat} ($0.046$), and
Cars/VISReg \texttt{supcon-ft-resnplm} ($0.049$); CIFAR-10 is the only dataset
whose grid ($\ldots,0.02,0.03,0.1$) brackets its own crossing, which is
precisely why its numbers are the trustworthy ones.

Three statements survive the caveat. First, \textbf{Flowers never reaches

champion space of each cell, at the existing 50 toys, would bracket every
crossing in an interval of width $\le0.02$ and turn Table~\ref{tab:reach} from
a set of bounds into a measurement. It requires no retraining --- only the
battery, on cached embeddings --- and it is the natural companion to the
coverage audit that produced these curves. We have not run it.
